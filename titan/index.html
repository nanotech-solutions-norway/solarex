SolarEX Titan TiO₂

Active TiO₂ photocatalytic surface engineering.

SolarEX Titan is a transparent TiO₂ nano-film engineered for UV-supported photocatalytic activity and hydrophilic/superhydrophilic rinse behavior on photovoltaic glass.

Photocatalytic pathway

The active pathway for UV-rich PV environments.

SolarEX Titan is designed for sites where UV irradiance can activate TiO₂ photocatalysis and where organic, biological or industrial fouling profiles are relevant to PV glass performance.

Titan is not a passive easy-clean route. Its technical value depends on UV-supported surface activation and the contamination types present at the site.

Hydrophilic rinse response

Mechanism

How Titan works.

Titan operates through TiO₂ photocatalysis. Under sufficient UV exposure, the surface generates reactive oxygen species that break down organic and biological contaminants and shift the surface toward hydrophilic/superhydrophilic behavior.

Active decomposition

Titan supports contaminant breakdown through UV-triggered TiO₂ photocatalytic activity. The mechanism is relevant for organic films, biofouling tendencies and industrial atmospheric residues.

Mechanism boundary

Titan must not be specified as a no-UV route. When UV availability is insufficient, Quartz is the preferred passive SiO₂ pathway for easy-clean behavior.

Best fit

Where Titan performs best.

Titan is the active UV pathway. It is preferred where photocatalytic decomposition is technically relevant and UV availability can activate TiO₂ surface behavior.

High-irradiance PV sites

Desert, tropical and high-sun conditions can support the UV input required for active TiO₂ behavior.

Organic contamination

Titan is relevant where organic films, biological residues, hydrocarbon traces or industrial atmospheric deposits are present.

Biofilm-prone environments

Where biological fouling risks exist, photocatalytic activity may provide a more suitable pathway than passive repellence alone.

Regular rinse/water exposure

Hydrophilic/superhydrophilic behavior is most useful when water can sheet across the surface after activation.

Process logic

Photocatalytic behavior sequence.

Titan’s behavior should be explained as a sequence: UV exposure activates the TiO₂ surface, reactive oxygen species act on organic residues, and the surface shifts toward water-sheeting behavior.

1

UV exposure

Ambient UV reaches the TiO₂-coated surface.

2

ROS generation

Activated TiO₂ generates reactive oxygen species at the surface.

3

Organic breakdown

Organic and biological residues are decomposed at the interface.

4

Hydrophilic rinse

Water sheets across the activated surface during rain or wash exposure.

Application engineering

Application and activation planning.

Correct application engineering is critical to Titan performance. The surface must be prepared correctly, and the site must have sufficient UV exposure for the active pathway to function as intended.

1

Clean

Remove dust, grime, soiling and residues, including surfactants and tensides.

2

Apply

Use HVLP spray or wipe/lint-free cloth according to approved Titan application instructions.

3

Cure

Allow cure and activation development according to source guidance; full effect may develop across approximately 24–48 hours.

4

Verify

Confirm uniform coverage and verify site conditions support UV-dependent functionality.

Pathway selection

When Titan is the correct choice.

Titan is preferred when UV availability, contamination type and rinse/water behavior support the active photocatalytic route. Quartz remains the correct pathway for passive UV-independent repellence.

Choose Titan when

Organic, biological or industrial soiling is relevant and sufficient UV exposure is available to support active photocatalytic behavior.

Review Quartz when

Dust, pollen, mineral deposits, low-UV conditions or passive easy-clean requirements dominate the site profile.

Technical FAQ

Titan — common technical questions.

Does Titan need UV light to function?

Yes. Titan’s active photocatalytic mechanism requires UV exposure. Without sufficient UV, Quartz is normally the more appropriate pathway.

What does photocatalytic mean?

Under UV activation, TiO₂ generates reactive oxygen species that support decomposition of organic contamination at the surface.

Does Titan replace cleaning?

No. Titan supports cleaner surface behavior and residue breakdown where conditions fit, but O&M schedules should be adjusted, not discontinued.

Is Titan better than Quartz for all sites?

No. Titan fits UV-supported active photocatalysis. Quartz fits passive UV-independent hydrophobic/oleophobic easy-clean behavior.
