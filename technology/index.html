Engineering principle

Surface coating selection is a technical decision.

Surface coating selection is not a procurement commodity choice. The mechanism by which a coating modifies glass behavior must be matched to the contamination profile, UV availability and operating environment of each site.

SolarEX’s two-pathway platform exists because no single surface chemistry addresses every fouling regime. Pathway selection is made upstream, informed by site data, not by generic product preference.

01

Optical interface protection

The glass-air interface is where irradiance is lost to soiling. Nanocoating modifies this interface at molecular level.

02

Adhesion-energy control

Surface energy determines how contaminants bond to glass. SiO₂ and TiO₂ films alter this through distinct mechanisms.

03

Environment-matched mechanism

Contamination varies by geography, climate and industrial context. Mechanism fit determines technical outcome.

Platform overview

One platform, two surface pathways.

SolarEX offers two mechanistically distinct products, Quartz and Titan, each engineered to deliver a defined surface function on transparent nano-scale films for photovoltaic glass.

Quartz — passive SiO₂

Passive repellence pathway

Quartz is a passive hydrophobic and oleophobic film that requires no UV activation. It targets inorganic soiling such as dust, pollen and mineral particulates by reducing adhesion energy.

  • 100–150 nm transparent film architecture.
  • Suited to high-latitude, low-UV and rainfall-assisted environments.
  • Surface repellence mechanism: reduces adhesion.

Titan — active TiO₂

Active photocatalytic pathway

Titan is a UV-triggered photocatalytic film. Under sufficient UV exposure, TiO₂ generates reactive oxygen species and supports a hydrophilic/superhydrophilic rinse response.

  • Designed for organic, biological and industrial contamination profiles.
  • Suited to higher-irradiance, desert, tropical and industrial environments.
  • Active decomposition mechanism: breaks down contaminants.

Surface science

What changes at the glass surface.

Both SolarEX products operate at the nano-scale interface between photovoltaic glass and its operating environment. The changes are measurable in surface energy, contact angle and adhesion behavior, not in bulk optical properties.

Surface energy modification

SiO₂ or TiO₂ chemistry changes the surface energy of glass and controls whether water beads or sheets.

Transparent nano-scale films

Quartz forms a 100–150 nm SiO₂ film. Titan forms a TiO₂ film only a few nanometers thick.

Adhesion behavior changes

Modified surface energy reduces work of adhesion, making rainfall or low-pressure rinse cycles more effective.

Passive repellence

Quartz prevents adhesion without altering contaminant chemistry.

Active decomposition

Titan uses UV-activated reactive oxygen species to break down organic molecules at the surface.

Product selection logic

Correct pathway selection requires UV, soiling, climate and cleaning-model review.

Product deep dive

Passive Quartz versus active Titan.

Quartz — passive SiO₂ film architecture

Quartz applies a 100–150 nm transparent SiO₂-based film to photovoltaic glass, modifying surface energy to produce hydrophobic and oleophobic response without dependency on UV availability or ambient irradiance levels.

Quartz does not require sunlight to function. Its mechanism operates through surface chemistry alone, reducing adhesion energy for inorganic particulates including mineral dust, pollen and atmospheric particulates.

Titan — active TiO₂ photocatalytic architecture

Titan applies a TiO₂-based photocatalytic film that is activated by UV irradiance. Upon sufficient UV exposure, the film generates reactive oxygen species that decompose organic and biological contaminants at the glass surface.

Following UV activation, Titan-coated glass transitions toward hydrophilic/superhydrophilic behavior, enabling water to sheet across the surface instead of bead.

Pathway selection

UV availability is the key decision divider.

The single most important variable in pathway selection is UV availability. This determines whether Titan’s photocatalytic mechanism can operate as intended and where Titan delivers its highest technical value.

Quartz — no UV required

Quartz operates independently of irradiance conditions. It is correct for high-latitude installations, seasonal low-irradiance periods, shaded arrays and dust/pollen-dominated soiling profiles.

Titan — UV activation required

Titan requires sufficient ambient UV to generate reactive oxygen species and trigger hydrophilic/superhydrophilic behavior. It fits high-irradiance, desert, tropical and industrial contexts.

Application engineering

Application is controlled surface engineering.

Film performance is directly dependent on application quality. Surface preparation, method consistency and cure management are engineering-grade requirements for field performance.

1

Prepare

Glass must be clean, dry and free of residual surfactants, cleaning agents or atmospheric contamination.

2

Apply

HVLP spray and cloth/wipe methods are supported. Uniform coverage is the critical outcome parameter.

3

Cure

Pre-cure is approximately 30 seconds. Full cure is approximately 24 hours; Titan full effect may develop across 24–48 hours.

4

Verify

Post-application visual inspection confirms coverage uniformity and identifies anomalous sheening or uneven distribution.

FAQ

Frequently asked technical questions.

What is the difference between easy-clean and active photocatalysis?

Easy-clean coatings reduce contaminant adhesion energy. Active photocatalytic coatings generate reactive oxygen species under UV and chemically decompose organic contaminants.

Does Titan work without UV?

No. Titan’s photocatalytic mechanism requires UV irradiance. Quartz is the appropriate specification in low-UV conditions.

Does Quartz require UV?

No. Quartz is a passive SiO₂ film with hydrophobic and oleophobic properties established at application.

Do coatings replace maintenance?

No. Quartz and Titan reduce cleaning burden by modifying adhesion and removal behavior. O&M schedules should be adjusted, not discontinued.

Does thicker coating improve performance?

Not necessarily. Performance is determined by chemistry, uniformity and surface preparation, not excessive thickness.
